Learning often starts with a small question. How does this work? Why was it built that way? What would happen if it were a little different?

Following one of those questions can be more rewarding than rushing to the next answer.

## Make a note of the question

A good question is worth keeping. Writing it down makes it easier to return to when there is time to explore.

The answer doesn't need to be complete. A short explanation, a useful example, or a link to a deeper source can be enough for a note to be helpful later.

## Learn by making

Reading gives an idea its shape. Building something with it reveals the parts that are still unclear.

- Try the smallest example you can think of.
- Change one detail and see what happens.
- Explain the result in your own words.
- Keep the questions that remain.

> A useful note doesn't have to be the final word. It can be the beginning of the next question.

## Keep a little room open

Not every interest needs a goal or a deadline. Sometimes the interesting thing is worth following simply because it is interesting.

Leave a little room for that.
